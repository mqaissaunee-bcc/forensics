01Overview — what this lab is really teaching

A smartphone is the most revealing evidence source most people carry: it knows where they've been, who they talked to, what they searched, what they installed, and when. This lab works a physical image of a Samsung Galaxy — the most complete kind of mobile extraction, including deleted data — and pulls out device identity, email, Google Maps navigation history, Wi-Fi and SIM details, call logs, installed apps, and SMS/MMS.

You'll use Autopsy with the Android Analyzer and Exif Parser ingest modules to both manually read the key databases/config files (settings.db, checkin.xml, the Gmail mailstore, da_destination_history, wpa_supplicant.conf, SimCard.dat) and review Autopsy's parsed call logs, installed programs, and messages — learning where every parsed artifact actually came from.

Two themes. First, extraction type determines what you get: a physical image reaches deleted space and is the gold standard, but most modern phones (encrypted, locked bootloaders) only permit logical or advanced logical extractions — still rich, but live-data-only. Second, phone evidence is almost all SQLite + XML config, organized by app package name — so, exactly like IoT (Lab 15), the skill is knowing which package's database holds IMEI, which holds Maps history, which holds the SIM's ICCID. Device identifiers (IMEI, Gmail account, SIM ICCID) are what tie the hardware, the account, and the subscriber together.

03Walkthrough

Task 1Open the data partition

Android physical images have many partitions; the user data lives in one.

  1. New case → add Lab16.001 → Configure Ingest → tick Exif Parser + Android Analyzer → Finish. Let ingest finish.
  2. Expand to Lab16.001 > vol15 (the data partition). Everything below is organized by app package name.

Task 2Read device identity and settings

Who the device is, and how it was configured.

  1. data/com.android.providers.settings/databases/settings.db → Application tab. System table (sound, airplane, driving mode); secure table (Bluetooth, data roaming, non-market app installs, lockscreen enabled, face unlock).
    📸Screenshot for your report: The secure settings table showing lockscreen/face-unlock state.
  2. com.google.android.gms/shared_prefs/checkin.xml (Text tab): the IMEI (unique device ID) and the associated Gmail account.
    📸Screenshot for your report: checkin.xml showing the IMEI and Gmail account.

Task 3Email, Maps, Wi-Fi, SIM

The behavioral and location goldmine.

  1. com.google.android.gm/databases/mailstore..db → messages (from/to, dateSentMs/dateReceivedMs → right-click Display as > Date, subject, snippet) and attachments (filename, linked by messageId).
  2. com.google.android.apps.maps/databases/da_destination_history → destination_history: navigation time, dest_lat/lng, dest_address, source_lat/lng — where the user navigated from and to.
    📸Screenshot for your report: The Maps destination_history row with coordinates and address.
  3. misc/wifi/wpa_supplicant.conf (Text): wireless interface, device model/manufacturer/serial, saved SSIDs + encryption + passphrases.
  4. System/SimCard.dat (Text): SIM ICCID, phone number, operator, country, and the Unix time a SIM was changed/removed — current and previous SIM.

Task 4Review Autopsy's parsed artifacts

See the Android Analyzer's normalized output and where it came from.

  1. Results → Extracted Content → Call Logs (source logs.db; to/from number, start/end times, direction, contact name).
  2. Installed Programs (name + install date — note any social-media apps).
    📸Screenshot for your report: The parsed Call Logs or Installed Programs list.
  3. Messages (direction, numbers, date, read status, subject, body, SMS vs MMS, thread ID, from-number).

04Reference — extraction types and where evidence lives

Mobile extraction types (most to least complete)

TypeGets youWhen
PhysicalFull flash incl. deleted/unallocated — this lab.Rooted/exploitable devices; the ideal.
Advanced logical (file system)Most app data + system files, live only.Common on modern devices.
LogicalContacts, calls, SMS, some app data via APIs.Default fallback.
ManualPhotos of the screen — last resort.When nothing else works.

Key identifiers — hardware, account, subscriber

  • IMEI (checkin.xml): uniquely identifies the handset. Dial *#06# on a live phone to display it.
  • Gmail/account (checkin.xml, mailstore): ties the device to a cloud identity.
  • SIM ICCID + phone number (SimCard.dat): identifies the subscriber; previous-SIM data shows swaps.
  • Wi-Fi MAC/SSIDs + Maps history: location evidence.

Where to look, by package

Evidence is foldered by app package: com.android.providers.settings (settings/IMEI area), com.google.android.gm (Gmail), com.google.android.apps.maps (navigation), com.sec.android.provider.logsprovider (calls). Know the package, find the database.

06Manual corrections & currency notes

The NDG manual was written in 2020–21. These are the places where it is wrong, inconsistent, or out of date — worth knowing before you trust a screenshot.

WhereThe manual saysWhat's actually true
Device vintageSamsung Galaxy S2-era NIST CFReDS imageOld. Modern Androids are encrypted by default with locked bootloaders, so a full physical image is often impossible without exploits or vendor cooperation — advanced-logical is the realistic ceiling. The analysis method holds; the acquisition is far harder now.
Android AnalyzerAutopsy's Android Analyzer moduleStill present, but real mobile exams lean on Cellebrite UFED, Magnet AXIOM, MSAB XRY, or ALEAPP/iLEAPP, which parse hundreds of apps. Autopsy is a capable free option; know the commercial tools exist.
iOS absenceAndroid-onlyiOS is roughly half the market and very different — encrypted backups, keychain, a different file system (APFS), and SQLite databases in distinct locations. Mobile forensics means both platforms; this lab covers one.
App schema driftReads specific table/column namesApp databases change schema between versions constantly — today's mailstore or Maps table may differ. Rely on the method (find the package → open its SQLite → identify the relevant table), not memorized column names.

07Making it defensible

Phones are the richest — and most contested — evidence; handle with matching rigor:

  • State the extraction type. Physical vs logical determines what's present and what absence means. Deleted data missing from a logical extraction isn't 'wasn't there.'
  • Separate handset, account, and subscriber. IMEI = device, Gmail = account, SIM = subscriber — and none of those is automatically 'the defendant.' Corroborate.
  • Convert and document timestamps. Mail-ms vs other epochs; state the zone.
  • Hash the image and exports (Lab 20); document source paths by package — schemas change, so provenance is how others reproduce you.
On the stand

'My client's phone said X' invites every attack: was the phone his, was the account his, was the SIM his, could someone else have used it, is the app data reliable at that version? Lead with device/account/subscriber identifiers, state the extraction type and its limits, and corroborate location and messaging with independent sources.

09Check yourself

1. Rank the mobile extraction types by completeness and say what the top one uniquely provides.

Physical > advanced logical (file system) > logical > manual. A physical image uniquely includes unallocated space and deleted data — the most complete picture — but is often impossible on modern encrypted, locked devices.

2. Where on an Android image do you find the IMEI and the device's Google account, and why do both matter?

In com.google.android.gms/shared_prefs/checkin.xml. The IMEI uniquely identifies the handset; the Gmail account ties it to a cloud identity — together they link the physical device to an account, a key step in attribution.

3. Why can't you rely on memorized table and column names when examining phone app databases?

App database schemas change between versions constantly, so a table or column present in one version may be renamed or gone in another. You rely on the method — find the app's package, open its SQLite database, identify the relevant table — rather than fixed names.

4. A phone's data implicates someone. Name three distinct identifiers you'd separate, and why.

IMEI (the handset), the Google/Gmail account (the cloud identity), and the SIM ICCID/phone number (the subscriber). None of them is automatically 'the person' — keeping them distinct, and corroborating each, is what makes attribution defensible.
