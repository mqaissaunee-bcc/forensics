01Overview — what this lab is really teaching

Every technique in this course produces nothing admissible without one thing: an unbroken, documented chain of custody. It's the record of who had the evidence, when, and what they did with it — from the moment of seizure to its appearance in court. A single undocumented gap can make the most damning forensic finding worthless. This capstone lab is where the technical work meets the legal reality.

You'll learn to identify and record a device correctly — reading the make, model, capacity, and above all the serial number off a hard drive, and the model number and IMEI off a phone — then document its physical condition, and fill out both an evidence intake form and a chain-of-custody form that tracks each transfer with names, signatures, dates, and times.

The core principles: identify by the most unique value available (serial number for a drive, IMEI for a phone — neither is ever duplicated), document condition before you touch anything (so pre-existing damage can't be blamed on you), and record every transfer (released-by, received-by, date/time, purpose — no gaps). The discipline sounds bureaucratic; it is the difference between evidence that stands and evidence that's thrown out.

Read every identifier and the physical condition before anything else.

  1. Open the exhibit photos for FOR_LAB_021_001. From the drive label record: Make (Hitachi), manufacture date, capacity (500GB), model (HDP725050GLA360), and — the anchor — serial number (RF3MRNEJ).

  2. Document physical condition from the other-angle photos: color, form factor (3.5-inch), and any marks — scratches on the top-left corner, a '3' written in blue marker. Note pre-existing damage so it can't later be attributed to your handling.

Task 2Identify and document the phone

Phones carry their IDs in different places — and the IMEI is the key.

  1. Open FOR_LAB_021_002. From the back of the Samsung Galaxy S7 record the model (SM-G930W8) and the IMEI (359118083705756). On a device with no printed serial, the IMEI is the unique identifier.
    📸Screenshot for your report: The phone's back showing the model and IMEI.
  2. Note: on a live phone you can display the IMEI by dialing *#06# — and you'd document that you did so and why.
  3. Record condition (no visible damage) from the second photo.

Task 3Fill out the intake form

The intake form opens the chain — who handed over what, when, and why.

  1. Open NDG Evidence Intake form.pdf. Enter case-ref FOR_LAB_021, mark First request, set the request date, request type Examination, submitting person/investigator (Det. John Brown), priority Normal, crime type Fraud, seizure location, dates needed / of search and seizure, and type of seizure (Warrant).
  2. In Details of evidence item(s), write exhibit 001 fully: 'Silver Hitachi Deskstar 500GB Hard drive, Model HDP725050GLA360, S/N RF3MRNEJ. Number 3 in blue ink, scratches on the side. Appears in good working condition.' — identifier + condition together.
  3. Summarize the examination request, then enter the submitting-person and lab-personnel names and signatures. Those signatures make the form a link in the chain. Save as FOR_LAB_021-YYYY-MM-DD Intake Form.pdf.
    📸Screenshot for your report: The completed intake form with evidence details and signatures.

Task 4Fill out the chain-of-custody form

The CoC form tracks every hand-off after intake — no gaps allowed.

  1. Open the CoC form. Enter case number, case type (Fraud), investigator name + ID, victim, suspect, date seized, and location.
  2. In the Description of Evidence table, enter each exhibit with its unique ID: 001 = 500GB Hitachi, Model HDP725050GLA360, S/N RF3MRNEJ; 002 = Samsung Galaxy S7, Model SM-G930W8, IMEI 359118083705756.
  3. In the Chain of Custody table, log the transfer: item #, date/time, released by (+signature), received by, and comments/location — one row per transfer, for every exhibit. Add a purpose comment ('Exhibit handed over to storekeeper').
    📸Screenshot for your report: The chain-of-custody transfer table with released-by/received-by entries.
  4. Enter the receiving name, signature, and date; save as FOR_LAB_021-YYYY-MM-DD CoC Form.pdf. Note the Final Disposal Authority section exists for documenting eventual disposal.

04Reference — what makes a chain unbreakable

Device identifiers, most to least unique

DeviceBest identifierAlso record
Hard drive / SSDSerial number (S/N) — never duplicatedMake, model, capacity, manufacture date
PhoneIMEI (globally unique, carrier-transmitted) — *#06# liveModel, serial if present, SIM ICCID
Any deviceThe most unique value printed/retrievablePhysical description + condition

The three disciplines

  • Identify uniquely. Anchor on the serial/IMEI; model and make corroborate but can be shared across many units.
  • Document condition first. Color, form factor, marks, and especially pre-existing damage — recorded before you handle it, so it can't be blamed on you.
  • Record every transfer. Each hand-off: released-by (+signature), received-by (+signature), date/time, purpose. An undocumented gap is a broken chain.

Where the forms sit in the lifecycle

The intake form opens the record at submission (who handed over what). The CoC form tracks every subsequent transfer. Your acquisition metadata from Lab 1 (examiner, drive serial, times) and your hashes from Lab 20 (proving the image never changed) are the technical evidence that the chain's integrity held — forms and hashes together make the whole record defensible.

06Manual corrections & currency notes

The NDG manual was written in 2020–21. These are the places where it is wrong, inconsistent, or out of date — worth knowing before you trust a screenshot.

WhereThe manual saysWhat's actually true
Form specificsUses NDG's particular PDF formsEvery agency and lab has its own forms and fields — don't memorize these exact boxes. The principles (unique ID, condition, every transfer signed/timed) are universal; the form is just the vehicle.
Digital chain of custodyPaper-style formsModern labs increasingly use digital evidence-management systems (barcoded, access-logged, tamper-evident) that auto-record transfers. The documentation burden is the same; the mechanism is electronic and harder to forge.
Hashing as chain integrityTreats forms as the whole chainThe technical half of custody is hashing (Lab 20): a stored acquisition hash proves the data never changed even as the device moved through hands. Forms track physical custody; hashes prove data integrity — you need both.
IMEI as identityRecords IMEI to identify the phoneThe IMEI identifies the handset, not the owner or the SIM — keep device/subscriber/person distinct (see Lab 16). Record the SIM ICCID too when present.

07Making it defensible

This lab is defensibility — it's the discipline that lets every other finding survive challenge:

  • No gaps, ever. Every transfer documented with who, when, and why. A single undocumented hand-off can exclude the evidence entirely.
  • Unique identification. Serial/IMEI anchors the paperwork to this exact device — so there's no 'how do we know it's the same drive?'
  • Condition on record from the start. Pre-existing damage documented up front protects you from allegations of mishandling.
  • Forms + metadata + hashes together. Intake/CoC forms (physical custody), Lab 1 acquisition metadata (who imaged it, when), and Lab 20 hashes (data unchanged) form one continuous, verifiable record.
On the stand

Chain of custody is the first thing the defense attacks, because if it breaks, nothing else matters — your brilliant analysis never reaches the jury. The complete, gap-free, uniquely-identified, signed record is what gets your evidence admitted in the first place.

09Check yourself

1. Why record a hard drive's serial number rather than just its model number?

Serial numbers are unique to each individual drive and never duplicated, so they anchor the paperwork to that exact device. Model numbers are shared across thousands of units and can't uniquely identify the specific exhibit.

2. Why must you document a device's physical condition — including existing scratches — before you handle it?

So pre-existing damage is on record from the start and can't later be attributed to your handling. Documenting condition up front protects both the evidence's integrity and the examiner against allegations of mishandling.

3. What must every entry in a chain-of-custody transfer log contain, and why does a missing entry matter so much?

Each transfer needs the item, date/time, who released it (with signature), who received it (with signature), and the purpose. A single undocumented gap breaks the chain — and a broken chain can render the evidence inadmissible, no matter how strong the forensic findings are.

4. How do the chain-of-custody forms and the hashing from Lab 20 work together to make evidence defensible?

The forms track physical custody — who held the device and when — while the acquisition hash proves the data never changed as it moved through those hands. Forms establish an unbroken physical chain; hashes prove data integrity along it. Together they make the whole record verifiable and admissible.
