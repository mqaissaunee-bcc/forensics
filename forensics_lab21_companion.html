Overview — what this lab is really teaching

Brilliant analysis is worthless if the evidence is thrown out. Chain of custody is the documented, unbroken trail showing who handled an item, when, and why — from seizure to court. This lab is the paperwork discipline that makes everything in the other 20 labs admissible: correctly describing seized devices and filling out the intake and chain of custody forms.

If Lab 01 was 'work on a copy,' this is the other pillar: document everything. A single gap in custody can sink a case no matter how strong the technical findings.

Walkthrough

Task 1 — Describe the devices

1 Read the hard-drive identifiers

From the exhibit photos: Make (Hitachi), manufacture date (Mar-2009), capacity (500 GB), Model (HDP725050GLA360), and Serial (RF3MRNEJ). Then record the physical description and condition — color, form factor, and any pre-existing marks (scratches on a corner, a '3' written in marker). Documenting prior damage protects you from later claims you caused it.

2 Read the phone identifiers

The Samsung Galaxy S7: Model (SM-G930W8) and IMEI (359118083705756) from the back. If identifiers aren't printed, dial *#06# to display them — and note that you did so.

IMEI vs serial

The IMEI uniquely identifies the handset and is transmitted to the carrier during calls/SMS — unlike a serial — so it links the physical phone to network records.

Task 2 — Fill out the intake form

3 Complete the intake

Case-ref (FOR_LAB_021), first-request flag, request date, request type (Examination), submitting person/investigator, priority, crime type (Fraud), seizure location, date needed, date of search & seizure, and type of seizure (Warrant). In Details of evidence item(s), write a full description: "Silver Hitachi Deskstar 500GB hard drive, Model HDP725050GLA360, S/N RF3MRNEJ; '3' written in blue ink, scratches on the side; appears in good working condition." Summarize the examination goals in the service request, then both parties sign.

Signatures make it a legal link

The moment the submitting person and lab personnel both sign, the intake form becomes a documented transfer in the chain. Most fields can be typed, but signatures should be genuine.

Task 3 — Fill out the chain of custody form

4 Record the custody transfer

Case number, case type, submitting person + ID, victim, suspect, date seized, location of seizure. In Description of Evidence, enter each item with an EID, quantity, and description (drive and phone). In the Chain of Custody table, log the transfer: item #, date/time, released-by, received-by, and comments/location — e.g. handing exhibits to the storekeeper. Name and signature at the bottom. Save with a meaningful name: FOR_LAB_021-YYYY-MM-DD CoC Form.pdf.

Why it matters in court

Custody is the foundation under everything else

A perfect forensic image, a decisive timeline, a cracked password — all inadmissible if the chain of custody is broken. This paperwork is not bureaucracy; it's what lets your technical work be heard at all.

  • Admissibility depends on an unbroken, documented trail.
  • Attribution — unique identifiers tie evidence to the specific seized device.
  • Integrity of handling — condition notes rebut 'you damaged it' claims.

Course relevance

Core to Module 1 (the profession & investigations) and Module 4 (processing crime and incident scenes). Know the identifiers to record, intake vs CoC forms, and why an unbroken chain is non-negotiable.

Check yourself

1. A seized phone has a model number and an IMEI. Which do you use as the primary identifier, and why?

The IMEI — it uniquely identifies this exact handset (and is transmitted to the carrier), whereas the model number only describes the device type.

2. Why document a device's pre-existing scratches and marks at intake?

To establish its condition before you took custody, rebutting any later claim that you damaged it.

3. What single failure can make even flawless technical findings inadmissible?

A broken chain of custody — a gap in the documented trail of who handled the evidence, when, and why.

4. Why is same-day submission of evidence to the lab considered best practice?

It minimizes the window for custody gaps and strengthens the chain against challenge.
