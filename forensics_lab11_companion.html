Overview — what this lab is really teaching

Every photo a phone takes can carry a hidden dossier: the camera make and model, the exact date and time, and often GPS coordinates. That's EXIF metadata, and this lab extracts it with ExifTool. Then it carves photos out of a Thumbs.db file — where Windows caches thumbnail copies that can survive even after the originals are deleted.

Tools & environment

DEFT for ExifTool (command line), then WinOS + HxD for Thumbs.db carving.

Walkthrough

Task 1 — Extract EXIF with ExifTool

1 Go to the photos and run ExifTool

On DEFT, open LXTerminal and:

deft@deft:~$ cd Evidence_Files/FOR_LAB_011/Pics
deft@deft:~$ exiftool IMG_001.jpg

Dump it to a readable file with exiftool IMG_001.jpg -w txt, then open the resulting .txt in gedit.

Linux filenames are case-sensitive

IMG_001.jpg ≠ img_001.jpg. Type exactly what ls shows.

2 Read the camera, time, and GPS

In the output: the Make/Model block, Date/Time Original, and — at the bottom — the GPS coordinates and altitude. Note them; repeat for IMG002–004 and compare the times and locations.

GPS ties a person to a place and time

A photo's embedded coordinates can place a device — and by inference a person — at a specific location at a specific moment. That's frequently decisive.

Task 2 — Carve photos from Thumbs.db

3 Open Thumbs.db in HxD

On WinOS, open E:\FOR_LAB_011\DB_File\Thumbs.db in HxD. Thumbs.db is a hidden system database of thumbnail images for a folder — created when the user viewed pictures as thumbnails.

4 Carve a JPEG by signature

Same carving technique as Lab 07: Find header FF D8 FF E0 (note offset), find footer FF D9 (note offset), Select block, Save selection as pic1.jpg. It'll be a small thumbnail of one of the photos — potentially one that was deleted from the folder. Continue carving the remaining thumbnails.

Reference — picture-file artifacts

SourceHoldsWhy it survives
EXIF (in the JPEG)Camera, timestamp, GPSTravels with the file wherever it's copied
Thumbs.dbThumbnail copies of folder imagesMay retain a thumbnail after the full photo is deleted
File-system datesCreate/access/modify on the volumeChange on copy/move — less reliable than EXIF for 'when taken'
EXIF vs file-system time

Date/Time Original records when the shutter fired; file-system dates record when the file landed on this volume. They often disagree — EXIF is usually the truer 'when it happened.'

Why it matters in court

Pictures place people

EXIF GPS + timestamp can put a device at a scene; Thumbs.db can prove a now-deleted image once existed in a folder. Together they recover both the what and the where/when even after a user tries to erase the originals.

  • EXIF for attribution — device model can be corroborated against a suspect's phone.
  • Thumbs.db for persistence — deletion of the photo doesn't erase the cached thumbnail.
  • Prefer EXIF time for when a photo was actually taken.

Course relevance

Central to Module 8: Recovering Graphics Files. Know EXIF fields (especially GPS), ExifTool basics, and Thumbs.db as a source of persistent thumbnails.

Check yourself

1. Three photos were deleted from a folder. How might you still prove what they showed?

Carve the cached thumbnails from the folder's Thumbs.db — it can retain thumbnail copies after the originals are deleted.

2. Which EXIF field best establishes when a photo was actually taken, and why prefer it over the file-system date?

Date/Time Original — it's written by the camera at capture, whereas file-system dates change when the file is copied or moved.

3. What does GPS EXIF data let an investigator establish?

The location where the photo was taken, which can place the device (and by inference a person) at a specific place and time.

4. ExifTool returns no GPS for a photo. What can and can't you conclude?

You can conclude there's no embedded location in that file; you can't conclude the device never had location on — it may have been off for that shot or stripped afterward.
